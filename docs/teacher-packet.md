## Learning objectives

Mapped to the strands of the National Standards for Personal Financial Education (Council for Economic Education / Jump$tart). By the end of the period students can:

- **Earning Income** — Explain how career choice, education, and skills drive earning power. *In Tycoon: students pick a career path (Technology, Healthcare, Skilled Trades, Finance, Creative, and more) and can invest in education — e.g. a $15K coding bootcamp for a 15% salary bump, or weigh an $80K bachelor's against starting work sooner.*
- **Spending & budgeting** — Build a monthly budget where income must cover rent, expenses, and debt payments before anything is left to invest. *In Tycoon: every turn is one month; cash flow is the constraint students feel immediately.*
- **Saving** — Distinguish saving (safety, liquidity) from investing (growth, risk). *In Tycoon: the ladder starts at a high-yield savings account (~4.5%, FDIC-insured, "start here") before riskier rungs open up.*
- **Investing** — Build a diversified portfolio and watch compounding over years. *In Tycoon: the ladder climbs from savings → bonds → an S&P 500 index fund → individual stocks → crypto (Bitcoin is flagged "extremely volatile, only invest what you can lose") → REITs → rental real estate with properly amortized mortgages.*
- **Managing Credit** — Understand borrowing, interest, and how debt shapes net worth. *In Tycoon: student-loan balances accrue interest, and home purchases compare FHA (3.5% down) vs. conventional 20%-down mortgages with the math shown line-by-line.*
- **Managing Risk** — Weigh probability and consequence; use diversification as a defense. *In Tycoon: 80+ life events — layoffs, recessions, medical bills, surprise car repairs, windfalls, scams — test whether a portfolio (and an emergency fund) can absorb a shock.*

## 45-minute run-of-show

1. **Hook / setup (~5 min).** Post the link. Each student picks a character (each starts with different cash, debt, and a career — e.g. a software engineer with savings, or a healthcare worker carrying $25K in student loans). Read the win condition aloud: *passive income ≥ 110% of expenses.* Frame the question: **"Can you make your money outearn your life?"**
2. **Play block 1 — the first year (~15 min).** Students set up a budget, take or upgrade a job, and make their **first investments**. Encourage one safe move (savings / index fund) and let them eye the riskier options. They'll advance month by month and feel the trade-off between spending, saving, and investing.
3. **Mid-point checkpoint (~5 min).** Pause everyone. Pulse question: **"What's your passive income right now, and what are your monthly expenses?"** Have three students call out their ratio. This makes the win condition concrete and surfaces who is investing vs. just hoarding cash.
4. **Play block 2 — ride a cycle (~12 min).** Keep advancing into a market swing and at least one **life event**. Some portfolios dip; some students will be tempted to panic-sell. Let them. The game is quietly recording it (see debrief). Tell them to keep playing toward year 3.
5. **Debrief (~8 min).** Close laptops or look up. Run the discussion questions below.

## Debrief / discussion questions

These tie directly to Tycoon's two built-in reflection features — the **Hindsight** and the **Year-in-Review** — plus core risk concepts.

1. **The Hindsight.** "When the market dropped and you sold an asset, the game later showed you what it would be worth 12 months on. What did your sold asset do — and what would you do differently next time?"
2. **The Year-in-Review.** "Your January report showed market gains vs. income you earned, and what your net worth would be if you'd *never* invested. Which did more for you this year — your salary or your investments?"
3. **Diversification.** "Did you put everything in one place, or spread it out? When the bad event hit, did that choice help or hurt?"
4. **Risk vs. reward.** "Compare the safe savings account to the riskiest thing you bought (crypto, a single stock). Was the extra risk worth it over the years you played?"
5. **Cash flow vs. net worth.** "You can have a big net worth and still be short on cash some months. Where did that bite you — and how does passive income change the picture?"

## Facilitation notes (the intended takeaways)

Short cues so you can steer the conversation without prep.

- **Hindsight →** Panic-selling locks in a loss and misses the recovery. The takeaway line: *time in the market beats timing the market.* A strong answer recognizes the emotional pull of selling and names a rule ("hold through dips," "only sell what I planned to") instead.
- **Year-in-Review →** For most students the **investments quietly out-earned the salary** over time — that's compounding doing the work. The "what if I never invested" number is the punchline; a strong answer connects small, consistent investing to a much larger ending net worth.
- **Diversification →** A concentrated bet swings hardest both ways. Strong answers see that **spreading money across uncorrelated assets softened the bad event** — diversification isn't about maximizing gains, it's about surviving shocks.
- **Risk vs. reward →** No universal "right" answer — the point is that **higher expected return came bundled with bigger swings**. A strong answer matches risk to time horizon and to how much loss they could stomach, not to greed.
- **Cash flow vs. net worth →** Wealth on paper doesn't pay rent. Strong answers distinguish **liquidity from net worth** and explain why an emergency fund and passive income (the win condition itself) create real stability.

## Ways to extend

- **Daily Challenge as a bell-ringer.** Everyone plays the **same seeded world and the same character on the same day** — identical market, identical events — so any difference in outcome is pure decision-making. It runs ~15 minutes and produces a **shareable result card** (final net worth). Drop it in as a recurring warm-up; it's a fair, self-contained class competition and a natural homework loop.
- **Journaling prompt.** After a run, have students write a half-page from their **Year-in-Review**: their best call, their worst call, and one rule they'd set for next year. Quick to assign, quick to grade.
- **"Who reached financial freedom first?"** Run a full game across a unit and track who first hits passive income ≥ 110% of expenses — or, on demo, who has the highest net worth at month 36. The result card makes the leaderboard run itself.
