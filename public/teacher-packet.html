Teach Tycoon in 45 minutes

A ready-to-run personal-finance lesson — students earn, invest, hit real life, and learn from their own decisions.

Play: tycoonjan22026.netlify.app · Educator details: /educators

Runs in any browser, including Chromebooks. No installs, no student accounts, no PII — one link gets the whole class in. The free demo covers the first 3 in-game years (36 months), enough for a full class period. Win condition: a student reaches financial freedom when passive income covers 110% of monthly expenses.

ALearning objectives

Mapped to the strands of the National Standards for Personal Financial Education (Council for Economic Education / Jump$tart). By the end of the period students can:

  • Earning Income — explain how career choice, education, and skills drive earning power. In Tycoon: students pick a career path (Technology, Healthcare, Skilled Trades, Finance, Creative, and more) and can invest in education — e.g. a $15K coding bootcamp for a ~15% salary bump, or weigh a bachelor's against starting work sooner.
  • Spending & budgeting — build a monthly budget where income must cover rent, expenses, and debt before anything is left to invest. Every turn is one month; cash flow is the constraint students feel immediately.
  • Saving — distinguish saving (safety, liquidity) from investing (growth, risk). The ladder starts at a high-yield savings account (~4.5%, FDIC-insured, "start here") before riskier rungs open up.
  • Investing — build a diversified portfolio and watch compounding over years. The ladder climbs from savings → bonds → an S&P 500 index fund → individual stocks → crypto (Bitcoin flagged "only invest what you can lose") → REITs → rental real estate with properly amortized mortgages.
  • Managing Credit — understand borrowing, interest, and how debt shapes net worth. Student-loan balances accrue interest; home purchases compare FHA (3.5% down) vs. conventional 20%-down mortgages with the math shown line-by-line.
  • Managing Risk — weigh probability and consequence; use diversification as a defense. 80+ life events — layoffs, recessions, medical bills, surprise repairs, windfalls, scams — test whether a portfolio and an emergency fund can absorb a shock.

B45-minute run-of-show

  1. Hook / setup (~5 min). Post the link. Each student picks a character (each starts with different cash, debt, and a career — e.g. a software engineer with savings, or a healthcare worker carrying ~$25K in student loans). Read the win condition aloud: passive income ≥ 110% of expenses. Frame it: "Can you make your money outearn your life?"
  2. Play block 1 — the first year (~15 min). Students set a budget, take or upgrade a job, and make their first investments. Encourage one safe move (savings / index fund) and let them eye the riskier options. They advance month by month and feel the spend-vs-save-vs-invest trade-off.
  3. Mid-point checkpoint (~5 min). Pause everyone. Pulse question: "What's your passive income right now, and what are your monthly expenses?" Have three students call out their ratio — it makes the win condition concrete and surfaces who's investing vs. just hoarding cash.
  4. Play block 2 — ride a cycle (~12 min). Advance into a market swing and at least one life event. Some portfolios dip; some students will be tempted to panic-sell. Let them — the game quietly records it (see debrief). Keep playing toward year 3.
  5. Debrief (~8 min). Look up. Run the discussion questions below.

CDebrief & discussion questions

Tied directly to Tycoon's two built-in reflection features — the Hindsight and the Year-in-Review — plus core risk concepts.

  1. The Hindsight. "When the market dropped and you sold an asset, the game later showed what it would be worth 12 months on. What did your sold asset do — and what would you do differently next time?"
  2. The Year-in-Review. "Your January report showed market gains vs. income you earned, and what your net worth would be if you'd never invested. Which did more for you this year — your salary or your investments?"
  3. Diversification. "Did you put everything in one place, or spread it out? When the bad event hit, did that choice help or hurt?"
  4. Risk vs. reward. "Compare the safe savings account to the riskiest thing you bought (crypto, a single stock). Was the extra risk worth it over the years you played?"
  5. Cash flow vs. net worth. "You can have a big net worth and still be short on cash some months. Where did that bite you — and how does passive income change the picture?"

DFacilitation notes — the intended takeaways

Short cues so you can steer the conversation without prep.

  • Hindsight → panic-selling locks in a loss and misses the recovery. The line: time in the market beats timing the market. A strong answer names the emotional pull of selling and a rule to resist it ("hold through dips").
  • Year-in-Review → for most students the investments quietly out-earned the salary over time — compounding doing the work. The "what if I never invested" number is the punchline.
  • Diversification → a concentrated bet swings hardest both ways. Strong answers see that spreading money across uncorrelated assets softened the shock — it's about surviving, not maximizing.
  • Risk vs. reward → no universal right answer — higher expected return comes bundled with bigger swings. Match risk to time horizon and to how much loss you can stomach.
  • Cash flow vs. net worth → wealth on paper doesn't pay rent. Strong answers distinguish liquidity from net worth and explain why an emergency fund and passive income create real stability.

EWays to extend

  • Daily Challenge as a bell-ringer. Everyone plays the same seeded world and character on the same day — identical market, identical events — so any difference in outcome is pure decision-making. ~15 minutes, with a shareable result card. A fair, self-contained class competition and a natural homework loop.
  • Journaling prompt. After a run, students write a half-page from their Year-in-Review: best call, worst call, one rule for next year. Quick to assign, quick to grade.
  • "Who reached financial freedom first?" Run a full game across a unit and track who first hits passive income ≥ 110% of expenses — or, on the demo, the highest net worth at month 36.
